# Dominion Rules Reference

Concise rules for Dominion (Donald X. Vaccarino; Rio Grande Games), current
through **Rising Sun** (2024), with notes on the announced **Arcana**. Card
wording follows current (second) editions and Dominion Online. A card's text
always overrides these general rules.

## Goal

Players start with identical small decks and build them during the game by
buying cards from a shared Supply. When the game ends, whoever has the most
victory points (VP) in their deck, plus any VP tokens, wins.

## Components and setup

- **Supply**: the piles players can buy from. It holds the Basic cards plus
  10 **Kingdom** piles of 10 cards each, which vary from game to game.
- **Kingdom Victory cards** (e.g. Gardens): 8 for 2 players, 12 for 3+.
- **Basic cards**:
  - Treasures: Copper ($0, makes $1), Silver ($3, makes $2), Gold ($6, makes $3).
    Copper is whatever remains after the starting decks; Silver 40; Gold 30.
  - Victory: Estate ($2, 1 VP), Duchy ($5, 3 VP), Province ($8, 6 VP).
    Estates and Duchies: 8 for 2 players, 12 for 3+, not counting starting
    Estates. Provinces: 8 / 12 / 12 / 15 / 18 for 2 / 3 / 4 / 5 / 6 players.
  - Curse ($0, −1 VP): 10 per player beyond the first.
- **Starting deck**: 7 Copper and 3 Estates, shuffled. Each player draws 5.
- **Trash**: one shared, face-up pile. Trashed cards leave the game.
- **Setup text**: text below a card's dividing line that begins "Setup:"
  changes setup, e.g. by adding piles or setting cards aside.
- **Landscape cards**: horizontal non-card rules objects such as Events,
  Landmarks, Projects, Ways, Allies, Traits and Prophecies. Random setups
  usually add 0–2 of them. Allies and Prophecies are added when their
  enabling cards are present (see below).

## Turn structure

Play goes clockwise. Each turn has phases in this order:

1. **Action phase**: you have 1 Action. Playing an Action card from your hand
   uses an Action. Follow its instructions fully, top to bottom, before
   playing anything else. "+N Actions" lets you play more Action cards.
2. **Buy phase**: you have 1 Buy. Play any number of Treasures, one at a time
   in any order, to produce $. Then buy cards. Each card costs one Buy plus
   its price, and "+N Buys" gives you more Buys. Unspent $ is lost at end of
   turn. **Once you buy anything (a card, Event or Project), you can't play
   more Treasures that turn.**
3. **Night phase** (Nocturne+): play any number of Night cards. No Actions
   are needed.
4. **Clean-up**: discard all cards from play and from your hand, except
   Durations that still have work to do. Draw 5 new cards. Your turn ends.

You don't have to play cards or buy anything. A player can't take more than
two turns in a row: an extra turn from Outpost, Mission, Possession, Fleet,
Voyage, Seize the Day etc. can't follow another extra turn.

## Core rules and terms

- **Deck / hand / discard pile / in play**: your draw pile is your *deck*.
  You can count your deck but not look through it. Only the top card of a
  discard pile is public.
- **Reshuffle**: when you need to draw, reveal or look at cards and your deck
  is empty, shuffle your discard pile to form a new deck. Then continue. If
  there still aren't enough cards, use what you have. Never shuffle at any
  other time. Cards still in play or being drawn aren't part of that shuffle.
- **Gain vs. buy**: a gained card goes to your discard pile unless the text
  says otherwise. Buying a card means paying for it and then gaining it.
  "When you buy" triggers only on buys. "When you gain" triggers on every
  gain, including buys.
- **Costs**: "costing up to $X" means it costs $X or less and has no Potion
  or Debt in its cost. Cost reductions never take a cost below $0. A card's
  cost can be compared with another's only when both have the same
  components: $, Potion and Debt.
- **Do as much as you can**: if an instruction can't be fully done, do as
  much of it as you can.
- **Optional instructions**: "you may" is optional. Everything else is
  mandatory.
- **Losing track**: if a card has moved somewhere unexpected, e.g. it was
  shuffled back into a deck, any instruction that tries to move "this" card
  again fails.
- **Simultaneous triggers**: if several things trigger at once, the current
  player resolves theirs first, then the others in turn order. Each player
  chooses the order of their own triggers.
- **Attacks**: affect other players in turn order. **Reactions** such as
  Moat are revealed in response, at the time the Reaction card states.
- **Empty pile**: an empty pile still exists and its cost can still be
  checked. It just can't be bought or gained from.
- **Non-Supply cards** (Prizes/Rewards, Spoils, Horses, Loot, Spirits,
  Travellers beyond Page/Peasant, etc.) live in piles outside the Supply.
  They can't be bought, and they don't count toward the three-pile ending.

## Card types

| Type | Behavior |
| --- | --- |
| Action | Played in the Action phase, using an Action |
| Treasure | Played in the Buy phase for $ (some have other effects) |
| Victory | Worth VP at game end. Usually dead in hand |
| Curse | −1 VP |
| Attack | Action that harms other players |
| Reaction | Can be revealed or used in response to a stated trigger, e.g. an Attack or a gain |
| Duration | Has effects on later turns. Stays in play until the Clean-up of the last turn it does something |
| Night | Played in the Night phase |
| Command | Plays a non-Command card from the Supply, leaving it there |
| Shadow | Can be played from your deck as if from your hand (see Rising Sun) |
| Omen | Has "+1 Sun" (see Rising Sun) |
| Liaison | Gives Favors (see Allies) |

A card can have several types (e.g. Action–Victory, Treasure–Duration–Loot)
and counts as each of them. If a card that plays another card (Throne Room
etc.) plays a Duration, it stays in play as long as the Duration does.

## End of game and scoring

The game ends **at the end of a turn** when either:

- the Province pile is empty (or the Colony pile, when Colonies are used), or
- **three** Supply piles are empty, or **four** with 5–6 players. A split or
  mixed pile counts as one pile and is empty only when all its cards are gone.

Count VP on every card you own, wherever it is: deck, hand, discard, in play,
set aside, mats or Exile. Add VP tokens and Landmark scoring. The highest
total wins. If tied, the tied player who took fewer turns wins. If still
tied, they share the win.

## Expansion mechanics

Each section lists only what the set *adds*. Sets are in release order.
Second editions replaced some cards (see the edition notes at the end).

### Base (2008; 2nd ed. 2016) and Intrigue (2009; 2nd ed. 2016)

- Base: the core rules above.
- Intrigue: multi-type cards (Action–Victory, Treasure–Victory,
  Action–Reaction) and "choose one" cards.

### Seaside (2009; 2nd ed. 2022)

- **Duration** cards, e.g. "now and at the start of your next turn".
- **Mats**, such as Native Village's and Island's, which hold cards set aside
  long-term. Cards on Island's mat still count for scoring.

### Alchemy (2010)

- **Potion**: a Basic Treasure ($4, makes one Potion) whose pile is used only
  with Potion-cost Kingdom cards. Potion costs need both $ and Potions.

### Prosperity (2010; 2nd ed. 2022)

- **Platinum** ($9, makes $5) and **Colony** ($11, 10 VP). These are often
  added when the Kingdom has Prosperity cards. With Colonies, an empty Colony
  pile also ends the game.
- **VP tokens** are kept on a player's mat and scored at game end.

### Cornucopia & Guilds (2011/2013; combined 2nd ed. 2024)

- **Variety** effects that reward differently named cards.
- **Rewards** (2nd ed.; they replace the 1st-ed. Prizes): a non-Supply pile
  gained by cards such as Joust.
- **Bane**: Young Witch adds an extra $2–$3 Kingdom pile whose cards block
  its Attack.
- **Coffers**: tokens you spend in your Buy phase for +$1 each.
- **Overpay**: "when you buy this, you may overpay" for an extra effect.
- Setup-text cards, e.g. Ferryman sets aside an unused pile.

### Hinterlands (2011; 2nd ed. 2022)

- Heavy use of **on-buy and on-gain** abilities, and reactions to gaining
  or discarding.

### Dark Ages (2012)

- **Shelters** (Hovel, Necropolis, Overgrown Estate) can replace the starting
  Estates.
- **Ruins**: a shuffled, non-Kingdom pile that Looters add to the Supply.
  Players are attacked into gaining Ruins.
- **Spoils**, **Madman** and **Mercenary** are non-Supply cards.
- **Knights**: a mixed pile of 10 different cards.
- **"When you trash this"** abilities, plus Command cards such as Band of
  Misfits.

### Adventures (2015)

- **Events**: landscapes bought with a Buy and $. They aren't cards, so they
  can't be gained, and they apply only when bought. Most can be bought more
  than once per turn unless they say otherwise.
- **Reserve** cards: when played they go on your **Tavern mat**. Later you
  **call** one at its stated timing and it does its effect. Called cards
  return to play and are discarded at Clean-up.
- **Travellers**: when discarded from play, you may exchange one for the next
  card in its line (Page → … → Champion, Peasant → … → Teacher).
- **Player tokens** placed on Supply piles: +1 Card, +1 Action, +1 Buy, +$1,
  −$1, −1 Card, −$2 cost, Journey, Estate and Trashing.

### Empires (2016)

- **Debt**: some cards cost Debt, e.g. 8D. When you **buy** one you take that
  many Debt tokens. Gaining it any other way doesn't. While you have Debt you
  can't buy anything. In your Buy phase, pay $1 to remove 1 Debt.
- **Split piles**: two different cards, 5 of each, with the cheaper half on
  top. Only the top card can be bought.
- **Castles**: a mixed pile ordered by cost.
- **Landmarks**: landscape cards that add scoring or VP-token rules.
- **Gathering** cards collect VP tokens on their pile.
- Also adds Events.

### Nocturne (2017)

- **Night phase** and **Night** cards.
- **Heirlooms**: each one replaces a starting Copper.
- **Boons** (on **Fate** cards): to "receive a Boon", turn over the top card
  of the Boons deck and follow it. **Hexes** (on **Doom** cards) work the
  same way, but other players receive them.
- **States** such as Deluded, Envious and Miserable belong to one player at a
  time.
- **Spirits** (Will-o'-Wisp, Imp, Ghost) and other non-Supply cards (Wish,
  Bat). **Zombies** start in the trash.

### Renaissance (2018)

- **Villagers**: tokens you spend in your Action phase for +1 Action each.
  Also uses Coffers.
- **Artifacts** (Flag, Horn, Key, Lantern, Treasure Chest): one holder at a
  time. Taking one takes it from whoever had it.
- **Projects**: landscapes each player may buy once. You mark yours with a
  cube and get a permanent effect.

### Menagerie (2020)

- **Horses**: a non-Supply pile (+2 Cards, +1 Action). A played Horse
  returns to its pile.
- **Exile** mat: Exiled cards stay out of your deck but still score. When you
  gain a card, you may discard all your Exiled copies of it.
- **Ways**: when you play an Action card, you may use the Way's text instead
  of the card's.
- Also adds Events.

### Allies (2022)

- **Favors** are tokens, and each player starts with 1. **Liaison** cards
  give Favors. When a Liaison is in the Kingdom, add one **Ally** landscape
  that turns Favors into effects.
- **Split piles of four**: four different cards with 4 copies each, ordered
  by cost (Augurs, Clashes, Forts, Odysseys, Townsfolk, Wizards). Only the
  top card is available. Some cards **rotate** the pile, moving all copies
  of the top card to the bottom.

### Plunder (2022)

- **Loot**: a shuffled, non-Supply pile of different $7 Treasures. "Gain a
  Loot" takes the top card.
- **Traits**: a landscape assigned to a Kingdom Action or Treasure pile.
  Every card in that pile gains the Trait's ability (e.g. Cheap, Cursed,
  Fated).
- Treasure–Duration cards. Also adds Events.

### Rising Sun (2024)

- **Omens** have "+1 Sun", which removes a Sun token from the **Prophecy**.
  Any game with Omens has one Prophecy, which starts with 5 / 8 / 10 / 12
  Sun tokens for 2 / 3 / 4 / 5 players. A Prophecy does nothing until its
  last Sun token is removed. Then it stays active for the rest of the game.
- **Shadow** cards: when you shuffle, put your Shadows on the bottom of the
  new deck. You can play a Shadow from your **deck** as if it were in your
  hand.
- Debt costs return. Also adds Events and setup-text cards (e.g. Riverboat).

### Arcana (announced June 2026; physical release expected Q4 2026)

Provisional: the full rules aren't public yet. Revisit this section once the
rulebook ships.

- Theme: "leveling up". **Studies** improve your cards and **Carts** improve
  your economy. Projects return.
- From the Dominion Online client: **Study** landscapes (e.g. Art of Memory,
  Hunting, Navigation), Action cards of the type **Learning**, Projects,
  and 5 Victory Kingdom piles. 37 Kingdom piles in total.

## Promos and special setups

- Promos include Black Market (its own deck of unused Kingdom cards), Envoy,
  Stash, Walled Village, Governor, Prince, Summon, Sauna/Avanto, Dismantle,
  Church, Captain and Marchland.
- Card setup rules override the defaults. Examples: Young Witch's Bane, Way
  of the Mouse's set-aside card, Druid's Boons, Obelisk's chosen pile, and
  Approaching Army's added Attack pile.

## Edition notes

Second editions removed these cards. They still appear in older games and
in Dominion Online.

- Base: Adventurer, Chancellor, Feast, Spy, Thief, Woodcutter.
- Intrigue: Coppersmith, Great Hall, Saboteur, Scout, Secret Chamber, Tribute.
- Seaside: Ambassador, Embargo, Explorer, Ghost Ship, Navigator, Pearl Diver,
  Pirate Ship, Sea Hag.
- Prosperity: Contraband, Counting House, Goons, Loan, Mountebank, Royal
  Seal, Talisman, Trade Route, Venture.
- Hinterlands: Cache, Duchess, Embassy, Farming Village, Ill-Gotten Gains,
  Mandarin, Noble Brigand, Nomad Camp, Oracle, Silk Road.
- Cornucopia & Guilds: Fortune Teller, Harvest, Horse Traders, Tournament
  (and its Prizes: Bag of Gold, Diadem, Followers, Princess, Trusty Steed),
  Doctor, Masterpiece, Taxman.
